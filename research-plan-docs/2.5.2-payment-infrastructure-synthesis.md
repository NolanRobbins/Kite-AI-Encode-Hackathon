## Paper 1: SoK: Blockchain Agent-to-Agent Payments

**ArXiv ID**: 2604.03733
**Authors**: Zhang, Xiang, Lei, Wang, Qiu, Sun, Zarkov, Yuen, Deppeler, Yu, Lam
**Date**: Apr 2026
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2604.03733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03733)
**Core Contribution**: First systematization of blockchain A2A payments. Defines a four-stage lifecycle (Discovery → Authorization → Execution → Accounting), categorizes x402 and peers, and identifies four fundamental failure modes. **Explicitly identifies price negotiation as a future direction.** Cites Kite whitepaper directly.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH — CRITICAL

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:

### The Four-Stage Lifecycle

| Stage | What Happens | Current Gap |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Discovery** | Identify payable operation, construct payment intent binding the obligation to its execution context | No regulation checks (jurisdiction, sanctions, licensing) before intent formation |
| **Authorization** | Determine admissibility under governance rules — delegated spending constraints, budget limits, policy controls | Doesn't capture execution history, cumulative spend, or multi-step strategies |
| **Execution** | Realize payment intent as concrete payment action; produce completion signal | Payment completion weakly coupled to service completion — financial finality ≠ fulfillment |
| **Accounting** | Link payment actions with originating intents and observed outcomes for reconciliation | Only post-hoc correlation between on-chain payment evidence and off-chain outcomes |

### Four x402 Failure Modes (Exact from Paper)

| Failure Mode | Description | NegotiatorGrid Impact |
|-------------|-------------|----------------------|
| **Weak Intent Binding** | Payments not reliably tied to a specific session, outcome, or verifiable fulfillment. A payment may still be valid when replayed, mismatched, or the service produces missing/partial/incorrect outcomes. | NegotiatorGrid's `deal_hash` (keccak256 of agreed terms) binds payment to a specific negotiation session. The `negotiation_id` in every message prevents replay. |
| **Misuse Under Valid Authorization** | Authorization validates transactions at submission but assumes trustworthy generation. If the agent is compromised via prompt injection, model manipulation, key leakage, or social engineering, adversaries generate valid-looking transactions. | NegotiatorGrid's Nash Validator checks that every proposed offer is game-theoretically rational — rejects offers that deviate from aspiration curve bounds, even if the LLM was manipulated into proposing them. |
| **Payment-Service Decoupling** | Payment completion ≠ service completion. In x402, completion is defined by transaction inclusion, not correct delivery. Providers can receive valid payment and not deliver. | NegotiatorGrid encodes SLA terms (latency, uptime, throughput) in the `DEAL_CONTRACT` message, which maps to Kite's on-chain SLA contracts with automatic penalty execution. |
| **Limited Accountability** | On-chain payment evidence loosely linked to off-chain outcomes via logs. Valid payments may lack verifiable evidence of successful delivery. | NegotiatorGrid's `welfare_attestation` block in the `DEAL_CONTRACT` records GlobalScore, buyer/seller utility, and Pareto distance — a quantitative on-chain record of outcome quality. |

### NegotiatorGrid as a Future Direction (Quoted from Paper)

> "We observe that payment terms may not always be fixed upfront. For example, agent-mediated interactions can involve iterative or multi-round negotiation over price, volume, or service scope, as reflected in evolving x402-style pricing models such as 'up-to' pricing and negotiated schemes. Therefore, lifecycle models should account for how payable conditions are formed across interactions, rather than assuming they are fully specified prior to authorization and execution."

This is the explicit academic validation that NegotiatorGrid fills a recognized gap. The paper lists **"Formation: negotiating payable terms across interactions"** as one of four future directions.

## Paper 2: A402: Binding Cryptocurrency Payments to Service Execution

**ArXiv ID**: 2603.01179
**Authors**: Li, Wang, Wang, Yang, Wang, Guan, Gao (Peking University)
**Date**: Mar 2026
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2603.01179](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01179)
**Core Contribution**: Identifies x402's atomicity gap (payment and service delivery are decoupled). Proposes Atomic Service Channels (ASCs) with TEE-assisted adaptor signatures that guarantee payment finalizes if and only if the service is correctly executed and the result is delivered.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:

### x402's Three Atomicity Failures

| ID | Failure | What Goes Wrong |
|----|---------|----------------|
| **L1** | Non-atomic execution/payment | Providers execute optimistically before payment finalization — exposed to non-payment. Facilitators broadcast payments without verifying execution. |
| **L2** | Non-atomic payment/delivery | x402 enforces payment-first: on-chain confirmation precedes result delivery. Malicious provider can withhold service after receiving payment. |
| **L3** | Scalability + privacy | End-to-end latency bounded by blockchain confirmation (~12.8s Solana, ~13min Ethereum). High per-tx fees make micropayments infeasible. All interactions visible on-chain. |

### How A402 Solves These (and what NegotiatorGrid can adopt)

**Atomic Service Channels (ASCs)**: Off-chain channels that embed service execution into payment state. The state `Γ = (B, ς)` tracks assets (client_free, client_locked, server_revenue) and status (OPEN, LOCKED, PENDING, CLOSED).

**The atomic exchange protocol (TEE + adaptor signatures)**:
1. Client submits request + locks payment δ → status LOCKED
2. Server executes in TEE → produces result `res`, samples secret `t`, encrypts `EncRes = Enc(res, t)`, creates adaptor pre-signature `ˆσ_S`
3. Client verifies pre-signature → sends conditional payment `σ_U` → status PENDING
4. Server reveals secret `t` → client decrypts result → payment finalized → status OPEN

The key insight: **revealing the secret that unlocks the payment also reveals the secret that decrypts the result**. Payment and delivery become mathematically atomic.

### Performance (vs x402)

| Metric | A402 | x402 (Ethereum) | Improvement |
|--------|------|-----------------|-------------|
| Throughput | 2,875 RPS | ~30 TPS | **95×** |
| Latency | ~350ms | ~13 min | **2,200×** |
| Cost (100 requests) | $3.35 | $95.02 | **28×** |

### NegotiatorGrid Adoption Path

NegotiatorGrid doesn't need to implement ASCs for the hackathon, but the architecture maps:
- NegotiatorGrid's **negotiation phase** happens before the ASC opens (determines the price δ)
- The ASC's **atomic exchange** replaces x402's facilitator-based settlement
- The **Liquidity Vault** aggregates multiple negotiated deals into one on-chain tx

For the hackathon MVP, continue using Kite's x402 facilitator. For production, migrate to ASC-style atomic settlement.

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "x402 decouples payment from delivery — a provider can take your money and not deliver. A402 proves this is solvable with atomic channels. NegotiatorGrid extends this by adding a negotiation layer before the payment channel opens."
**Code Repo**: None public (Peking University implementation)
**Limitations / Gaps**: Requires TEE infrastructure (AMD SEV-SNP). Not yet integrated with Kite or any L1. No negotiation protocol.

## Paper 4: Whispers of Wealth — Red-Teaming AP2

**ArXiv ID**: 2601.22569
**Authors**: Debi, Zhu
**Date**: Jan 2026
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2601.22569](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22569)
**Core Contribution**: First red-team attack on Google's Agent Payments Protocol (AP2). Introduces two attack techniques — **Branded Whisper Attack** (manipulates product ranking) and **Vault Whisper Attack** (extracts sensitive user data) — both via prompt injection against a Gemini-2.5-Flash + ADK shopping agent.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH (security)

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:

### The Two Attacks

| Attack | Mechanism | Impact | NegotiatorGrid Defense |
|--------|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| **Branded Whisper Attack** | Indirect prompt injection via merchant product descriptions. Adversarial text embedded in product metadata causes the LLM to preferentially rank the attacker's product. | Agent purchases attacker's product instead of the optimal one. In negotiation context: adversarial counterparty could inject prompts into their offer's `natural_language` field to bias the opponent's LLM toward accepting unfavorable terms. | **Nash Validator guardrail**: Every offer the LLM proposes or accepts is checked against the aspiration curve and NBS. Even if the NL field contains adversarial prompts, the structured `offer.price` must pass the game-theoretic check. The structured action token (`### OFFER(price=X) ###`) is what the protocol enforces — the NL field is untrusted display text. |
| **Vault Whisper Attack** | Direct prompt injection that causes the agent to leak its private data (budget constraints, user preferences, session tokens). | Attacker learns the agent's reservation price, BATNA, and spending limits — catastrophic information leakage that destroys negotiation leverage. | **Structural isolation**: NegotiatorGrid's `NEGOTIATE_REQUEST` message separates public fields (capability_needed, SLA requirements) from private fields (budget_range, reservation_value) in the system prompt. The private fields never enter the shared negotiation context. The `<mental_model>` block is computed locally and only its hash is transmitted. |

### Security Properties NegotiatorGrid Must Enforce

1. **NL ≠ Protocol**: The `natural_language` field in PROPOSE messages is display-only. The structured `offer` fields are what the protocol parses and enforces. This prevents prompt injection via NL from affecting the actual negotiation outcome.

2. **Private value isolation**: Budget ranges, reservation prices, and aspiration levels are never sent to the counterparty or included in any message content. They stay in the local agent's system prompt and Nash Validator.

3. **Deterministic validation**: The Nash Validator's checks (is_in_zopa, is_near_aspiration, is_above_reserve) are deterministic arithmetic — not LLM-based. Prompt injection cannot bypass arithmetic bounds checks.

4. **Structured action extraction**: Following AgenticPay's pattern, the `### OFFER(price=X) ###` token is parsed by regex, not by the LLM. The LLM generates it, but a deterministic parser extracts the values.

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "Simple prompt injection can manipulate LLM payment agents into buying the wrong product — NegotiatorGrid's game-theoretic validator catches adversarial offers that bypass LLM reasoning."
**Code Repo**: None
**Limitations / Gaps**: Only tests AP2 shopping flow, not bilateral negotiation. Does not propose formal mitigations — only identifies vulnerabilities.

## On-Chain Attestation Patterns — Implementable for Hackathon

### Pattern 1: Deal Hash Attestation (from Papers 1, 5)

```solidity
// Commit deal hash on-chain via session key metadata
addSessionKeyRule(
    sessionKeyAddress,
    agentId,
    sessionIndex,
    valueLimit,        // = negotiated price
```

### Pattern 2: Welfare Attestation (from Paper 1 + AgenticPay formula)

```python
# Compute welfare at deal time
welfare = {
    "buyer_utility": rb,           # (pmax - p) / (pmax - pmin)
    "seller_utility": rs,          # (p - pmin) / (pmax - pmin)
    "global_score": gamma**(t-1) * (D + 4*rb*rs*W + E),
    "pareto_distance": 0.02,
    "rounds": 12,
    "deal_hash": "0xkeccak256..."
}
# Include in DEAL_CONTRACT and optionally store on ServiceRegistry
```

### Pattern 3: ZK-Provable ZOPA Membership (from Paper 5)

```
Prove: price ∈ [pmin, pmax] without revealing pmin or pmax
Method: Commit to pmin, pmax via Pedersen commitments
        Use range proof to show p ≥ pmin AND p ≤ pmax
        Verifier checks commitments on-chain
```

This is aspirational for hackathon but the correct production path. For the demo, simply commit the `deal_hash` and `welfare_attestation` to Kite chain.
