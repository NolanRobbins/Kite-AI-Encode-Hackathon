## Paper 1: AgenticPay

**ArXiv ID**: 2602.06008
**Authors**: Liu, Gu, Song (UC Berkeley)
**Date**: Feb 2026
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2602.06008](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06008)
**Core Contribution**: First comprehensive multi-agent negotiation benchmark with 111 tasks across 8 market configurations and 10 business scenarios. Introduces structured action extraction (`### BUYER_PRICE($X) ###`) and welfare scoring (`GlobalScore = γ^(t-1) × (D + 4·rb·rs·W + E)`).
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:
- Adopt the `### PRICE($X) ###` token format for reliable LLM → protocol bridge
- Use the `GlobalScore` welfare formula as NegotiatorGrid's on-chain quality attestation — the `(p, pmax, pmin, t)` tuple is compact and ZK-provable
- Claude Opus 4.5 leads (86.9 score, 3.7 rounds, 100% deal rate); Llama-3.1-8B fails 48.6% of the time — use frontier models only
- The `<mental_model>` chain-of-thought block forces opponent reasoning before each bid

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "Claude Opus 4.5 closes deals in 3.7 rounds with 100% success rate, while sub-10B models fail nearly half the time — NegotiatorGrid uses frontier models with game-theoretic guardrails."
**Code Repo**: [github.com/SafeRL-Lab/AgenticPay](https://github.com/SafeRL-Lab/AgenticPay)
**Limitations / Gaps**: No opponent modeling from offer history; no LP-based optimization; welfare metrics assume scalar price (not multi-issue)

## Paper 2: ASTRA

**ArXiv ID**: 2503.07129
**Authors**: Kwon, Hae, Clift, Shamsoddini, Gratch, Lucas (USC/ICT)
**Date**: Mar 2025 (EMNLP 2025)
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2503.07129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.07129)
**Core Contribution**: Hybrid LLM→LP→LLM pipeline where LP solver generates Pareto-optimal offers, LLM handles preference inference and NL generation. Introduces Fairness×Stance classification that maps to λ parameter controlling selfishness vs cooperation.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:
- The LP solver adds +3.59 points over pure LLM (p < 0.001) — provably optimal offers beat LLM guesswork
- Stance classification (Generous/Neutral/Greedy) maps directly to NegMAS aspiration exponents: generous→conceder(0.25), neutral→linear(1.0), greedy→boulware(4.0)
- The Virtual Partner Agent (VPA) pattern scores candidate offers for acceptance probability before sending
- Human experts rated ASTRA offers 0.93 points higher on strategicness (p < 0.001)

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "ASTRA proved that LP-optimized offers achieve 17% higher scores than pure LLM negotiation — NegotiatorGrid combines this with on-chain settlement."
**Code Repo**: [github.com/DSincerity/ASTRA-NegoAgent](https://github.com/DSincerity/ASTRA-NegoAgent)
**Limitations / Gaps**: Limited to 3-item CaSiNo domain; LP formulation is pre-specified; no x402/blockchain integration

## Paper 3: LLM Rationalis?

**ArXiv ID**: 2512.13063
**Authors**: (2025, arXiv Dec 2025)
**Date**: Dec 2025
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2512.13063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13063)
**Core Contribution**: Mathematical framework for modeling concession dynamics using a hyperbolic tangent curve. Introduces two novel metrics: **burstiness (τ)** — timing of concession bursts — and **Concession-Rigidity Index (CRI)** — fraction of negotiation time spent in rapid concession. Large-scale human vs. LLM comparison.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:
- **CRI formula**: `CRI = 1 - 1.32 / (|a| × T)` where `a` is the slope of the tanh fit and `T` is total rounds. CRI ∈ [0,1]: 1 = brief intense concession burst (rigid), 0 = steady concession throughout
- LLMs systematically anchor at ZOPA extremes regardless of context or leverage — they optimize for fixed points, not dynamic strategy
- Humans show CRI ≈ 0.64-0.72 (strategic patience); GPT-4.1-mini CRI = 0.008 (no rigidity = over-compliance)
- "The ability of LLMs to negotiate does not improve with better models" — devastating finding that validates the need for game-theoretic guardrails
- Active Listening & Empathetic Probing used by 30% of humans but <5% of LLMs — biggest strategy gap

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "Current LLMs anchor at ZOPA extremes regardless of context — NegotiatorGrid's game-theoretic validator prevents this failure mode."
**Code Repo**: None found
**Limitations / Gaps**: Focuses on single-issue price negotiation only; no multi-issue extension; tested only GPT-4.x family

## Paper 4: NegotiationArena

**ArXiv ID**: 2402.05863
**Authors**: Bianchi, Chia, Yuksekgonul, Tagliabue, Jurafsky, Zou (Stanford)
**Date**: Feb 2024 (ICML 2024)
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2402.05863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05863)
**Core Contribution**: Three negotiation scenarios (ultimatum games, trading games, price negotiations) showing that LLMs are vulnerable to behavioral manipulation — "pretending to be desolate and desperate" improves payoffs by 20% against standard GPT-4. Quantifies irrational behaviors.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:
- **Manipulation vulnerability is real**: behavioral tactics (desperation, guilt-tripping) shift LLM negotiation outcomes by 20% — NegotiatorGrid's Nash Validator must defend against this by checking offers against utility-theoretic bounds regardless of NL persuasion
- LLMs exhibit human-like irrationalities: sunk cost fallacy, anchoring bias, loss aversion
- Multi-turn flexible dialogue is critical — single-round negotiations miss strategic depth
- Framework supports ultimatum, trading, and price negotiation — reusable for NegotiatorGrid testing

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "LLM negotiators are 20% exploitable by manipulation tactics — NegotiatorGrid's game-theoretic guardrails prevent emotional exploitation."
**Code Repo**: Referenced in paper, Stanford group
**Limitations / Gaps**: No structured bid format; pure NL; no opponent modeling; no on-chain settlement

## Paper 5: Game-Theoretic LLM

**ArXiv ID**: 2411.05990
**Authors**: Hua, Liu, Li, Amayuelas, Chen, et al.
**Date**: Nov 2024
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2411.05990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05990)
**Core Contribution**: Designs game-theoretic workflows (Dominant Strategy Search, Backward Induction, Bayesian Updates) that guide LLM reasoning. Without workflows: 0-10% Nash equilibrium rates. With workflows: 90-100%. Critical finding: negotiation makes LLMs cooperate too much (deviate from NE toward Pareto-optimal but strategically weaker outcomes).
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:
- LLMs without workflow achieve NE only 0-10% of the time in strategic games — validates the need for NegotiatorGrid's Nash Validator
- With game-theoretic workflow: 90-100% NE achievement — the structured workflow is what makes LLMs rational
- Negotiation paradox: "all models except o1 adopt Pareto-optimal strategy of mutual cooperation, which deviates from Nash Equilibrium" — LLMs over-cooperate
- o1 (reasoning model) is uniquely robust — maintains strategic rationality even under negotiation pressure
- Deal-or-No-Deal tested with incomplete information and Bayesian updating workflow

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "Without game-theoretic guidance, LLMs find Nash equilibria less than 10% of the time — NegotiatorGrid's pricing engine ensures every offer is strategically rational."
**Code Repo**: [github.com/Wenyueh/game_theory](https://github.com/Wenyueh/game_theory) (Apache 2.0, 99 stars)
**Limitations / Gaps**: Tested on stylized games (Prisoner's Dilemma, etc.), not realistic pricing negotiation; no integration with negotiation protocols or payment systems

## Paper 6: Cooperation, Competition, and Maliciousness

**ArXiv ID**: 2309.17234
**Authors**: Abdelnabi, Gomaa, Sivaprasad, Schönherr, Fritz (CISPA/Max Planck)
**Date**: Sep 2023 → NeurIPS 2024 (Datasets & Benchmarks Track)
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2309.17234](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17234)
**Core Contribution**: Multi-agent, multi-issue, semantically rich scorable negotiation games. Tests cooperation, competition, and malicious (greedy/adversarial) players. Shows GPT-3.5 and small models mostly fail; even GPT-4 underperforms in non-cooperative settings.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: HIGH

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:
- Scorable negotiation (quantifiable metrics per deal) is the right evaluation framework — aligns with NegotiatorGrid's welfare attestation
- Adversarial/greedy opponents significantly degrade cooperative agents — validates the need for robust strategies (boulware/TFT)
- Multi-issue complexity is where LLMs struggle most — arithmetic + inference + planning + dynamic multi-turn is extremely challenging
- Procedures to create new games and increase difficulty → reusable scenario generation methodology

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "Even GPT-4 underperforms in non-cooperative multi-issue negotiation — NegotiatorGrid's decoupled opponent model + optimizer architecture handles what pure LLMs cannot."
**Code Repo**: [github.com/S-Abdelnabi/LLM-Deliberation](https://github.com/S-Abdelnabi/LLM-Deliberation)
**Limitations / Gaps**: No structured bid extraction; no payment/settlement integration; no blockchain connection

### Paper 8: Competition and Cooperation of LLM Agents in Games

**ArXiv ID**: 2604.00487
**Authors**: (Apr 2026)
**Date**: Apr 2026
**URL**: [arxiv.org/abs/2604.00487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00487)
**Core Contribution**: Studies LLM agents in Cournot competition and resource allocation games. Finds LLM agents tend to cooperate when given multi-round context (converge to social welfare maximum, not Nash equilibrium). Chain-of-thought analysis reveals "heuristic mirroring" — agents assume opponents have identical preferences.
**Relevance to NegotiatorGrid**: MEDIUM

**Key Takeaways for Implementation**:

- LLMs converge to Pareto front when given appropriate strategic context — confirms ASTRA's finding

**Quotable for Demo/Judges**: "LLM agents naturally mirror their own preferences onto opponents — NegotiatorGrid's frequency-based opponent model learns the actual counterparty preferences."
**Code Repo**: None found

### The Unified Finding Across All Papers

Every paper independently confirms the same gap: **LLMs can negotiate conversationally but cannot optimize strategically.** They anchor at extremes, over-cooperate, and are exploitable by manipulation. The solution architecture converges across the literature:

```
LLM (preference inference + NL generation)
    ↕
Optimizer (LP solver / aspiration curve / Nash Validator)
    ↕
Structured Protocol (FIPA-CNP / SAOP / x402)
```

NegotiatorGrid implements exactly this three-layer stack.
